# Social Strategy

This is the top-level plan: which channels, what for, how to win, and at what cadence. It sits above content pillars (what themes) and the content calendar (what posts) and tells them where to point. Everything downstream inherits the choices made here.

The core idea, and what separates strategy from a to-do list:

> **Strategy is choices and trade-offs — above all, what *not* to do.** "Be on every platform, post consistently, engage your audience" is not a strategy; it's a list of platitudes that spreads a brand thin and wins nowhere. A real strategy picks the few channels where the audience is and the brand can win, deliberately drops the rest, and commits to one clear objective and a differentiated angle.

## When to use this

- Starting a social presence from scratch, or resetting a scattered one.
- Deciding which platforms to be on (and which to quit).
- Before defining content pillars or a calendar, to set the objective and channels they serve.

**When NOT to use it:** if a current strategy for this brand exists and is sound, load it and move on unless the user wants to revisit.

## Step 0 — Read the foundation first

Load any brand profile, audience research, and content pillars that exist for this brand. Strategy is downstream of *who you are*, *who you're for*, and *what you talk about* — without those it's guesswork. Ask for or draft the missing ones first where it matters.

## Step 1 — Set one primary objective (tied to the business)

Pick **one** primary objective social must serve right now — awareness, audience growth, community, authority/positioning, leads, or sales — chosen from what the **business actually needs** (a pre-launch startup needs awareness/audience; an established one needs leads/sales). Add at most one secondary. Define a **meaningful metric**, not a vanity one, and be honest about measurement (see "Goals & Measurement" below).

## Step 2 — Select channels (focus, don't spread)

The central decision. Score each candidate platform on four dimensions (see "Channel Selection" below):

1. **Audience presence** — is the target audience actually there?
2. **Format fit** — does the brand's natural content strength suit it? (don't lead with TikTok if the brand won't make video)
3. **Goal fit** — does the platform serve the objective?
4. **Capacity fit** — can the brand sustain quality there?

Pick the **1–3** that win on all four. **Name what you're deliberately dropping** (or running as repurpose-only maintenance). Most brands should start with **one** primary channel.

## Step 3 — Define the strategic wedge

How does *this* brand win on its chosen channels, given everyone else? The wedge comes from the brand's POV and positioning — a differentiated angle, a distinctive format, a consistent stance — not "post good content" (that's table stakes, not a wedge). See "Operating Model" below.

## Step 4 — Set the operating model

A strategy you can't sustain is a wish. Define (see "Operating Model" below):

- **Cadence** per chosen channel — realistic, matched to capacity; consistency over volume.
- **The content engine** — how foundation → pillars → plan → produce → repurpose → schedule fit together.
- **Phasing** — Phase 1 nail one channel; expand only once it works. Don't launch everywhere.
- **Capacity/roles** — who makes and who engages, and how much time.

## Step 5 — Write the artifact

Produce the strategy document: the objective + metric, the chosen channels (and the dropped ones, with reasons), the wedge, the operating model and phasing. Summarize back and invite edits.

## Quality bar — self-check

- Is there **one clear objective**, tied to the business (not "grow followers" for its own sake)?
- Are **channels chosen by the fit framework**, with the dropped ones named? (Not "all of them.")
- Is there a real **wedge** — a differentiated way to win, from the brand's POV?
- Is the operating model **sustainable** and **phased** (start focused, then expand)?
- Is measurement **honest** — meaningful metrics from the platforms' native insights (or a connected data source such as Windsor.ai for Instagram/Facebook), and no metric promised that no source has?
- Would following this make the brand *choose* — or just do everything everywhere?

If the "strategy" doesn't say no to anything, it isn't one.

## Edge cases

- **"We should be on every platform"** → push back: focus beats spread; pick where the audience is and the brand can win; name what to drop or run as maintenance.
- **Wrong-format pressure** (wants TikTok but won't/can't make video) → match channels to the brand's real content strengths, or plan to build the capability first.
- **No clear business goal** → help define one from the brand's stage; a strategy without an objective optimizes nothing.
- **Already spread thin across 6 platforms** → recommend consolidating to the 1–2 that work; it's often a *subtraction* strategy.
- **B2B vs B2C** → channel and goal differ sharply (LinkedIn/X authority+leads vs IG/TikTok awareness); use the audience profile.
- **Measurement expectations** → measurement comes from native insights or a connected data source; don't promise dashboards you can't deliver.

## Channel Selection — focus, don't spread

Most brands fail here by trying to be everywhere and being mediocre everywhere. The fix is a real framework and the discipline to say no.

> It is better to dominate **one** platform than to be forgettable on five. Most brands should start with **one primary channel** (maybe one secondary), nail it, and only then expand.

### The four-dimension fit test

Score each candidate platform on all four. A channel earns a place only if it's strong on all of them — especially the first two.

1. **Audience presence** — Is the target audience actually active and reachable here? If they're not here, nothing else matters.
2. **Format fit** — A team that won't or can't make video shouldn't lead with TikTok; a visual product shines on Instagram/Pinterest; a written-insight brand shines on LinkedIn/X.
3. **Goal fit** — Leads → LinkedIn; evergreen traffic → Pinterest/YouTube; awareness → TikTok/IG; real-time community → X.
4. **Capacity fit** — Every platform is a standing commitment, not a free checkbox.

Pick the **1–3** that win across all four. Then explicitly **name what you're dropping** — or demote it to "repurpose-only maintenance".

### Platform strengths

Use as a starting map; the audience and goal decide, not the platform's hype.

- **Instagram** — visual brand-building, lifestyle, community; broad reach via Reels.
- **TikTok** — discovery/awareness through short video and trends. Demands video.
- **LinkedIn** — B2B authority, leads, personal/founder brand; rewards insight and dwell.
- **X / Twitter** — real-time conversation, thought leadership, tech/media/niche communities.
- **YouTube** — long-form authority + searchable evergreen; Shorts for reach. High production commitment.
- **Pinterest** — evergreen search and traffic; strong for commerce, design, food, DIY, home, weddings.
- **Facebook** — older audience, local/community, Groups; still huge for local businesses.
- **Threads** — casual, conversational, IG-adjacent; lighter commitment.
- **Bluesky** — smaller text-first communities; usually a secondary/repurpose channel.

### When to add or drop a channel

- **Add** only after the primary channel is working *and* there's capacity — never to "have a presence."
- **Drop / demote** when the audience isn't there, the format doesn't fit, or quality can't be sustained. A dead or sloppy account is worse than no account.
- **Reassess** when the audience or goal shifts.

### The subtraction strategy

For a brand already spread thin, the strategy is often **removal**: consolidate to the 1–2 that actually work, put the rest on maintenance, and pour the freed-up effort into winning where it counts.

## Goals & Measurement

### Pick one primary objective

- **Awareness / reach** — get discovered. (Early brands, new products.)
- **Audience growth** — build an owned following to nurture.
- **Community / engagement** — deepen the relationship with existing followers.
- **Authority / positioning** — become the name people associate with the topic.
- **Leads** — capture interest (email, demo, DM, waitlist).
- **Sales / conversion** — drive purchases directly.

Don't default to "more followers" — ask what the *business* needs social to do.

### Meaningful metrics vs vanity metrics

| Vanity (often) | Meaningful (usually) |
|---|---|
| Follower count | Saves, shares/sends |
| Likes | Comments / DMs / replies |
| Impressions alone | Profile visits → clicks |
| "Going viral" once | Leads, sign-ups, sales, repeat reach |

A post with 200 saves and 10 DMs usually beats one with 5,000 likes and no action.

### The funnel reality — social warms, it rarely closes

Social mostly **warms** people through awareness → trust → consideration; conversion often happens elsewhere (site, email, DMs, sales call). Judge the *body of work*, match the metric to where the channel sits in the funnel, and remember a conversion goal still needs upper-funnel content feeding it.

### Honest measurement

Measurement comes from **platform-native insights** (Meta Business Suite, etc.) or a connected data source such as Windsor.ai; if neither is available it is **manual** — read each platform's insights, track the primary metric in a simple sheet, and review monthly rather than chasing every post. Don't promise dashboards or automated reporting you can't deliver.

### A simple goal statement

> "Over the next quarter, social exists to **{objective}**, measured primarily by **{metric}**, on **{channel(s)}**."

Everything else — pillars, plan, cadence — should serve that sentence.

## Operating Model — making the strategy run

### The strategic wedge

The **wedge** is the differentiated reason this brand wins attention, and it comes from the brand's POV and positioning. Usually one of:

- **A distinct point of view** — a stance the brand consistently takes that others won't.
- **An underserved angle** — "the only one teaching X *for* Y".
- **A signature format** — a recognizable, repeatable content form the brand owns.
- **A voice/personality** — a way of sounding that stands out in a beige feed.

If you can't articulate why this brand wins beyond "we'll post good stuff," the positioning needs sharpening first — surface that.

### The content engine

```
Foundation (brand profile · voice · audience)
      ↓
Pillars                ← the themes
      ↓
Plan                   ← the calendar
      ↓
Produce                ← the posts
      ↓
Repurpose              ← multiply
      ↓
Schedule               ← ship
      ↓
Engage                 ← the relationship
```

### Cadence

**Consistency beats volume**: a steady, modest cadence sustained for a year builds more than a burst that collapses in a month. Derive cadence from capacity, not ambition.

### Phasing

- **Phase 1 — Win one channel.** Commit a sustainable cadence and get genuinely good at it.
- **Phase 2 — Extend efficiently.** Add a secondary channel, fed largely by repurposing.
- **Phase 3 — Expand deliberately.** Add channels only as capacity and results justify.

### Capacity & roles

Who creates content, who engages/replies, how much time per week; how much is net-new vs repurposed. Plan to the time that actually exists.

## Examples — strategies that make real choices

### 1. B2B SaaS — payroll tool for small-business owners
- **Objective:** leads (demo sign-ups) + authority. Metric: demo requests + DMs.
- **Channels:** **LinkedIn** (primary, founder-led) + **X** (secondary). **Drop:** TikTok, Pinterest, Instagram, YouTube long-form.
- **Wedge:** radical transparency + the "payroll without a specialist" POV.
- **Cadence:** LinkedIn 3×/week (founder), X ~daily, repurposed from the same atoms.

### 2. Local — neighborhood coffee shop
- **Objective:** local awareness + community. Metric: saves, story replies, "saw it on IG" mentions.
- **Channels:** **Instagram** (primary) + **Facebook** (secondary — older/local + Groups). **Drop:** LinkedIn, X, TikTok (unless the owner enjoys it).
- **Wedge:** the human, neighborhood angle — the owner's real voice, regulars by name.
- **Cadence:** IG ~4×/week, FB cross-posted/adapted.

### 3. Ecommerce / DTC — sustainable sneakers
- **Objective:** awareness → sales. Metric: link clicks + sales.
- **Channels:** **Instagram** + **TikTok** (primary) + **Pinterest** (evergreen search traffic). **Drop:** LinkedIn.
- **Wedge:** design-first sustainability — looks good first, eco is the bonus.
- **Cadence:** IG/TikTok several×/week (much repurposed), Pinterest steady evergreen pins.

### 4. Personal brand — indie maker
- **Objective:** audience growth + authority. Metric: followers *that engage* + email sign-ups.
- **Channels:** **X** (primary) + **LinkedIn** (secondary). **Drop:** the rest, at least in Phase 1.
- **Wedge:** build-in-public with *real numbers*.
- **Cadence:** X daily-ish, LinkedIn 2–3×/week repurposed.

Each example names a single objective, picks 1–3 channels by fit and explicitly drops the rest, has a wedge, and keeps a sustainable cadence. A strategy that doesn't drop anything isn't choosing — and a strategy that doesn't choose isn't a strategy.
